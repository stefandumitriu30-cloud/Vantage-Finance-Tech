ERP Trends 2027–2030: A CFO Guide

Between 2027 and 2030 the finance ERP stops only recording transactions and starts executing work within rules set by finance. At the same time three deadlines arrive that cannot be postponed: the end of SAP ECC mainstream maintenance, IFRS 18 and EU e-invoicing under ViDA. This guide sets out the five trends that matter and the decisions to take in the next 12 months.

Gartner expects finance teams using cloud ERP with embedded AI assistants to close the books 30% faster by 2028, and AI-enabled solutions to account for 62% of cloud ERP spending by 2027, up from 14% in 2024 (Gartner). The value will go to companies with a clean, well-governed core, not to those that buy the most agents.

1. From recording to executing: agentic ERP

In 2026 the two main enterprise finance vendors moved AI from assistance to execution. What matters for a CFO is not the chat interface but the fact that the agent works inside the process, with the permissions and approval hierarchies already configured.

Where to look first in finance:

  • Credit and collections: agents that read customer payment behaviour and adapt dunning and priorities, with a direct effect on DSO.
  • Reconciliations and close: automatic matching, exception handling and a guided close checklist.
  • Accounts payable: document capture, three-way match and invoice blocks, where tools such as SAP VIM remain the natural entry point.
  • Planning: agents that detect signals, simulate scenarios and update plans downstream.

Both vendors present these agents as working within guardrails: they move routine work forward and bring exceptions and decisions to people. Full autonomy is not the 2027 scenario.

2. Data is the real project

An agent working on poor master data makes mistakes faster. Gartner's ERP predictions for 2026 warn that more than 70% of purchased ERP AI capabilities could become shelfware by 2030 (Gartner via Rimini Street). The vendors know it: SAP made Business Data Cloud the data foundation for its agents and added Reltio master data management to it.

  • Master data as a finance KPI: measure customers, suppliers, chart of accounts and cost centres (duplicates, missing fields, orphan records) with the same discipline as DSO.
  • Clean core: fewer modifications in the core mean faster upgrades and access to new AI features, which in SAP require S/4HANA and a clean-core architecture.
  • End-to-end process owners: if nobody owns the close from first to last step, automation reproduces today's confusion.
  • Shared definitions: one definition of margin, cash and working capital across ERP, EPM and the data platform, or different agents will give different answers to the same question.

In migration projects this becomes a simple rule: data cleansing starts before process design, not during testing. See our phase zero deliverables list for where the data assessment fits.

3. Governing AI agents: an internal control topic

An agent that posts a journal entry, releases a payment or clears an item is a new actor in the internal control system. Forrester recommends treating agentic ERP as a control design programme before an automation programme: every action needs evidence of who approved it, which identity executed it, what data it used and who owns the outcome (Forrester). Gartner lists AI trust, risk and security management among the five emerging themes in cloud ERP finance.

Four questions for the risk committee:

  1. Autonomy: what can the agent do alone, what needs managerial review, what must be escalated?
  2. Segregation of duties: are agent identities part of the SoD matrix and the periodic access reviews?
  3. Audit evidence: can the auditor rebuild the input, rule and approval behind every agent-generated entry?
  4. Cost: do consumption-based models have caps, alerts and a budget owner?

Accountability for the numbers stays with the company, not with the vendor or the algorithm.

5. Agentic suite or composable stack: also a contract decision

Finance leaders are split between an ERP core that becomes an integrated agentic platform and a composable stack of best-of-breed tools connected by APIs (PYMNTS). Large groups usually end up hybrid: a standard finance core, with treasury, consolidation, e-invoicing or AP automation in specialised components. SAP has opened its data layer to third-party agents through the Model Context Protocol, and Oracle lets customers combine its own, partner and external agents in AI Agent Studio.

What to negotiate at the next renewal:

  • AI pricing: consumption or credit models modelled by finance, with usage scenarios, caps and alerts.
  • Customer-side telemetry: agent usage and performance data available to you, not only to the vendor.
  • Portability: the right to use third-party agents and models and to export data and rules without penalties.
  • Proof of value: reference cases and measured KPIs in production, not only demos.
  • Migration credits: for ECC customers, conversion credits shrink with each year of delay, so the negotiation calendar should match the project calendar.

The CFO agenda for the next 12 months

The priority is not buying agents but preparing the ground on which agents can work safely.

Within 90 days

  • Take stock: ERP release, maintenance status, modifications in the core, contracts expiring.
  • Run a master data quality assessment (customers, suppliers, chart of accounts) with measurable metrics.
  • Start the IFRS 18 gap analysis on the chart of accounts and MPMs: the 2026 comparative is already running.
  • Map e-invoicing obligations in every country where the group invoices.
  • Name an owner for each end-to-end process: record-to-report, order-to-cash, procure-to-pay.

Within 6 months

  • Decide the ERP path (greenfield, brownfield, selective, or extended maintenance as a bridge) with a business case that includes migration credits.
  • Choose one or two low-risk, high-volume agentic use cases, such as collections or bank reconciliation, with baseline KPIs measured.
  • Agree the control model for agents with internal audit: autonomy levels, SoD, evidence, escalation.

Within 12 months

  • Put the first agents into production with a human in the loop and measure them against the baseline (DSO, days to close, automatic match rate).
  • Build IFRS 18 and e-invoicing into the migration roadmap to avoid double work.
  • Renegotiate contracts with clauses on AI pricing, telemetry and portability.

The indicators that matter: days to close, DSO, share of supplier invoices with no manual touch, share of automatic reconciliations, master data quality. If an AI investment does not move one of these numbers, review it.
