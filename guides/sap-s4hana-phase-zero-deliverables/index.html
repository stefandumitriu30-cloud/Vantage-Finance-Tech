SAP S/4HANA Phase Zero Deliverables List

A checklist of the 16 deliverables a phase zero (SAP Activate Discover phase) should produce before an SAP S/4HANA programme is mobilised: what each one answers, who owns it, and the exit criteria for the investment decision.

What is phase zero in SAP S/4HANA?

Phase zero is the work done before delivery starts, corresponding to the Discover phase of SAP Activate. Its purpose is a well-founded decision: whether to move to SAP S/4HANA, with which transition approach, at what scope, cost and timeline, and with which partner. Programmes that skip it usually make the same decisions later, in Explore or Realize, when changing course is far more expensive.

It sits immediately before Prepare in the SAP S/4HANA implementation roadmap.

Use the list as a checklist. Not every item weighs the same in every programme: the Readiness Check and custom code analysis matter most for a brownfield conversion, while a greenfield programme puts more weight on scope and data.

Phase zero exit criteria

Phase zero ends with an investment decision by the executive sponsor or steering committee. Before that meeting, check that:

  • the business case is approved by Finance, with benefits tied to KPIs that will be measured after go-live;
  • the transition approach and deployment model are decided and the reasoning is documented;
  • scope is defined at the level of processes, organisational units and countries, with explicit exclusions;
  • the budget includes contingency and internal effort, not just partner fees;
  • the release plan respects fixed external dates such as e-invoicing mandates, contract expiries and the end of mainstream maintenance for the current ERP;
  • the executive sponsor, business process owners and programme manager are named and have the time to do the job.

How long does phase zero take, and who is involved?

Six to twelve weeks is typical. A small core team does the work: a programme manager or PMO lead, a solution architect, a technical lead for the readiness and code checks, the main business process owners (finance, procurement, sales, supply chain) and someone from Finance for the business case. External advisors often help with the assessments; independence from the eventual implementation partner is an advantage here.

Common phase zero mistakes

  • Choosing the partner before the approach. The partner's preferred method then decides greenfield versus brownfield.
  • A business case built only on IT cost. End of maintenance is a reason to move, but it does not make a case for process change.
  • Ignoring data until Realize. Data quality is the most common cause of delay in mock loads; assess it now.
  • Scope without exclusions. Unstated assumptions return as change requests.
  • No named business owners. Without them, design sign-off in Explore stalls.

What happens after phase zero

The Prepare phase turns the decision into a running programme: charter, governance, the integrated plan and the RACI. Our SAP S/4HANA RACI matrix covers who does what from Prepare to Run, and the Modern PMO guide covers PMO set-up in more depth.
