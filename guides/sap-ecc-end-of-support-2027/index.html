SAP ECC End of Support 2027: Options, Costs and Timelines

Mainstream maintenance for SAP ECC 6.0 ends on 31 December 2027. ECC keeps running, but what SAP covers shrinks step by step until 2030, when database, Java and conversion support end as well. This guide sets out the real dates, the five options and what each means for the finance function.

The dates that matter

At the end of 2024 only 39% of SAP's roughly 35,000 ECC customers had licensed S/4HANA, according to Gartner figures reported by The Register, and half of the German-speaking user group DSAG expects to run ECC past 2027. A full migration typically takes 18 to 36 months, so for many companies the question is no longer whether to bridge, but how.

What actually stops in 2027 and 2030

Nothing switches off. The risk is gradual: each phase covers less, and the customer carries more operational and compliance risk.

  • Mainstream maintenance (to 2027): fixes, security patches, legal and regulatory updates.
  • Extended maintenance (2028–2030): similar scope for EHP 6 to 8, at a higher fee and with possible technical limitations.
  • Customer-specific maintenance: help on known problems, but no new legal changes and new issues may be charged as consulting. For finance this is the critical point: a new VAT rule, an e-invoicing mandate or a change to statutory reporting will not arrive as a standard update.

The 2030 database and Java cut-off matters even for companies planning to stay on ECC: a system on Oracle, IBM Db2 or Microsoft SQL Server loses SAP's end-to-end database support, and a later conversion to S/4HANA is no longer possible from that database.

Two practical bridges are often overlooked by finance teams:

  • SAP Central Finance: replicating ECC postings into an S/4HANA finance system gives group reporting, IFRS 18 layouts and new analytics before the operational ECC systems are migrated.
  • Separating the decisions: covering the maintenance gap with a bridge and negotiating the migration on a calm timeline, instead of signing under deadline pressure.

What it means for finance

An ECC migration is often run as an IT project, but most of the design decisions land in finance:

  • Universal Journal: FI and CO merge into one table (ACDOCA), which changes reconciliation, reporting and the close.
  • Ledgers and parallel accounting: the choice between ledger and account approaches for IFRS and local GAAP, and whether to activate document splitting.
  • New Asset Accounting: mandatory in S/4HANA, with depreciation areas mapped to ledgers.
  • Business Partner: customers and suppliers become business partners (CVI): a master data project in its own right.
  • Accounts payable add-ons: solutions such as SAP VIM run on both ECC and S/4HANA, but the migration is the moment to review VIM version, workflow and approval rules, and to move approvers to Fiori.
  • Regulatory timing: IFRS 18 applies from 2027 and ViDA e-invoicing from July 2030. Delivering them twice, once on ECC and again on S/4HANA, is the most expensive path.

Planning backwards from 2030

With 18 to 36 months of delivery, a programme that must be live before the end of 2030 needs its decision in 2027 and its start no later than 2028. A practical sequence:

  1. Now: confirm release, enhancement package and database; inventory every SAP system (ECC, CRM, SRM, SCM); check contract and maintenance terms.
  2. Within 6 months: run a phase zero: business case, Readiness Check, custom code and data assessment, transition approach.
  3. By end-2027: decide extended maintenance or another bridge, and sign the migration on terms you have modelled.
  4. 2028 – 2030: deliver the migration along the S/4HANA implementation roadmap, aligned with IFRS 18 and e-invoicing deadlines.
