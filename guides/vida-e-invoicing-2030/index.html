ViDA 2030: EU E-Invoicing and Digital Reporting Explained for Finance and ERP Teams

From 1 July 2030, invoices for intra-EU B2B supplies must be structured e-invoices, and each transaction must be reported digitally to the tax authorities. The EC Sales List disappears. This guide explains what ViDA requires, the timeline to 2035, what changes for Italian companies and what to prepare in SAP and Oracle.

What ViDA is

VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) is the EU package adopted in March 2025 that modernises VAT in three pillars:

  1. Digital reporting and e-invoicing: structured e-invoices and transaction-level reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies from 1 July 2030.
  2. Platform economy: short-term accommodation and passenger transport platforms become deemed suppliers, optional from July 2028 and mandatory from January 2030.
  3. Single VAT registration: wider use of the One-Stop Shop and reverse charge, reducing the need to register for VAT in several countries.

For finance and ERP teams, pillar 1 is the one that changes processes and systems.

The 2030 rules in detail

TopicRule from 1 July 2030
FormatStructured electronic invoice based on the European standard EN 16931 (a PDF does not qualify).
Buyer acceptanceNot required: the supplier can issue e-invoices without the customer's consent.
Issuing deadlineWithin 10 days of the chargeable event (the original 2022 proposal said 2 working days; the adopted text says 10). For payments on account, 10 days from receipt.
Summary invoicesStill allowed under conditions, issued within 10 days after the end of the month; Member States may exclude them in fraud-sensitive sectors.
Supplier reportingTransaction by transaction, when the invoice is issued or should have been issued.
Buyer reportingIn principle the customer also reports the transaction, within 5 days of receiving the invoice; Member States have options on how this applies.
EC Sales ListAbolished and replaced by digital reporting. Correct reporting remains a condition for the intra-EU exemption. Intrastat remains, on its own legal basis.
Additional dataBeyond today's EC Sales List content, more fields are required, including bank details to help tax authorities trace payments.

What changes for Italy

Italy is the most advanced country in Europe on domestic e-invoicing, but its model is not the ViDA model:

  • Format: FatturaPA is not natively compliant with EN 16931; SdI and FatturaPA must become interoperable with the European standard.
  • Architecture: Italy uses centralised clearance through SdI, while ViDA's cross-border reporting is decentralised, with data exchanged between national systems and the central VIES.
  • Timing: Italy's current derogation runs to 31 December 2027, and pre-existing national systems must converge by 2035. Italy has adopted the national transposition and opened a public consultation; implementing decrees will set the detailed obligations.
  • Practical impact: intra-EU flows that today go through the esterometro and the EC Sales Lists (Intrastat) will move to e-invoicing plus digital reporting, with tighter deadlines and more data per invoice.

What to prepare in the ERP

  1. Master data: VAT numbers validated against VIES, country codes, customer and supplier addresses, bank details, and a clean classification of goods and services.
  2. EN 16931 mapping: check that the ERP can generate compliant UBL or CII files for every invoice type, including credit notes, self-billing and triangulation.
  3. Invoice timing: billing runs and approvals must issue the invoice within 10 days of the chargeable event; summary and periodic billing need review.
  4. Connectivity: a Peppol access point or a certified provider, integrated with SAP Document and Reporting Compliance or Oracle Fusion Tax and the Collaboration Messaging Framework.
  5. Accounts payable: receiving and validating structured invoices, and supporting buyer-side reporting where required; for SAP this is where tools such as SAP VIM come in.
  6. Reconciliation: transaction-level reports must match the VAT return and the ledger; plan monitoring and correction processes.
  7. Roadmap: gap analysis in 2026–2027, design in 2028, testing with the new central VIES from 2029, go-live by 1 July 2030, ideally combined with the SAP ECC decision.
