What is SAP VIM?

SAP VIM (Vendor Invoice Management by OpenText) automates the accounts payable invoice cycle inside SAP ECC and SAP S/4HANA: invoice capture, data recognition, validation, exception handling, approval and posting.

The key point to grasp early is that VIM is not an external system. It runs inside SAP in the /OPT/ namespace, uses standard SAP workflow and writes directly to MM and FI documents. No reconciliation between systems is needed: an invoice posted by VIM is a normal SAP document, with the invoice image always available.

SAP VIM architecture in five layers

The solution reads from top to bottom, following the path of the invoice:

  1. Capture channels – paper and scanning, email, structured e-invoices from tax authority platforms (such as Italy's SDI), EDI/IDoc, supplier portals and Ariba Network.
  2. Capture and OCR – OpenText Intelligent Capture classifies the document, extracts header and line-item data, proposes the vendor and purchase order and passes them to validation.
  3. VIM core – Document Processing – the DP document drives the process through process types, business rules, exception handling and the Chart of Authority.
  4. SAP S/4HANA / ECC – posting in MM-LIV for PO invoices (three-way match) or in FI-AP for non-PO invoices, with archiving through ArchiveLink.
  5. Reporting and analytics – VIM Analytics, KPI dashboards, aging, productivity and audit trail.

Document Processing: the core of VIM

Every incoming invoice creates a DP document that passes through five steps: creation, process type determination, checks through business rules, handling of any exceptions, and posting in SAP.

Process types

The process type decides which flow the invoice follows, based on the input channel, company code and document type (PO invoice, non-PO invoice, credit memo).

Business rules

Automatic checks for duplicates, tax data and VAT number, amount consistency and purchase order validity. An invoice that passes all of them can reach posting with no human touch.

VIM roles

Each step is assigned to a VIM role that works its own queue: Indexer/Validator, AP Processor, Approver/Coder, Buyer/Requester, AP Manager and VIM Administrator.

Exceptions and approval workflow

The goal of VIM is that only problem invoices need a person. The most common exceptions are price or quantity differences against the purchase order, goods receipt not yet posted, duplicate invoices or invoices from unknown vendors, incomplete tax data and a missing PO.

For non-PO invoices, the Chart of Authority determines approvers automatically by amount, company code and spend category. Coding and approval can also be done on the web or in Fiori, with automatic escalation and substitutes when someone does not act in time.

The SAP VIM approval workflow, step by step

The approval workflow is where most VIM projects succeed or stall. It runs on SAP Business Workflow, but the logic is configured in VIM rather than built from scratch, so the design work is about rules and responsibilities, not code.

Non-PO invoices: coding and approval

  1. Coding – the requester or an AP clerk enters the cost assignment: G/L account, cost center, internal order or WBS element, and tax code.
  2. Approver determination – the Chart of Authority (CoA) finds the approvers from the invoice attributes: company code, amount, expense type and organisational unit. Each approver has a limit; above it, the invoice moves to the next level.
  3. Approval – approvers act from the VIM Workplace, the web approval portal or the Fiori app on S/4HANA. Levels can be sequential or parallel, and an approver can approve, reject, send back to the coder or forward a question.
  4. Final check and posting – once the last required level has approved, the invoice returns to AP for a final check, or posts automatically if the process option allows it.

PO invoices: resolving blocks

PO invoices usually need no approval, because the purchase order already carries it. The workflow is triggered by discrepancies instead: a price variance goes to the buyer, a missing goods receipt goes to the requester or the warehouse, and a quantity variance to whoever can confirm what was delivered. When the cause is resolved, the invoice posts, or is released from its payment block through MRBR or the automatic release run.

Design choices that matter

  • Who maintains the CoA – approver limits change with every reorganisation. Give the business an owner and a maintenance process, otherwise invoices queue up on people who have left.
  • Substitutes and escalation – define who covers absences, and after how many days an untouched work item is escalated or reminded.
  • Thresholds – a single approval level for low-value invoices can remove a large share of the workload without weakening control.
  • Audit trail – every action, comment and user is logged on the DP document, which is what internal and external auditors will ask to see.

Integration with SAP FI and MM

VIM does not have its own accounting. Every invoice ends up as a standard SAP document, so the result depends as much on the FI and MM customizing underneath as on VIM itself.

PO invoices in MM-LIV

PO invoices are posted in logistics invoice verification (MIRO or BAPI) with the three-way match between purchase order, goods receipt and invoice. The settings that drive the outcome are the tolerance limits for price and quantity variances, GR-based invoice verification on the PO item, the GR/IR clearing account and the MM duplicate invoice check. When a tolerance is exceeded the invoice posts with a payment block, which VIM then manages as an exception.

Non-PO invoices in FI-AP

Non-PO invoices are posted in FI-AP (FB60 or BAPI) with the cost assignment entered during coding. VIM often parks the document first (MIR7 for logistics invoices, FBV1 for FI invoices) so it exists in SAP while approval is still running, and posts it once approved. Document types, number ranges and field status groups for vendor invoices need to support this.

E-invoicing into VIM: the Italian SDI example

Where e-invoicing is mandatory, incoming invoices arrive as structured files rather than paper or PDF. In Italy, supplier invoices arrive as FatturaPA XML through the Sistema di Interscambio (SDI). The file is received through a recipient code, an accredited intermediary or certified email (PEC), then read by SAP Document and Reporting Compliance (eDocument) or a localization add-on, which creates the DP document with the data already structured.

The gain is clear: no OCR, complete data extraction, fewer errors and a much higher share of invoices posted with no manual touch. The visual rendering of the invoice stays archived through ArchiveLink, while SDI notifications and legal archiving go through the archiving service. The same pattern applies to other clearance and Peppol-based mandates; see our e-invoicing implementation guide and the S/4HANA localization guide.

VIM Workplace and SAP VIM transactions

The VIM Workplace (transaction /OPT/VIM_WP) is where users spend their day. It shows each user the invoices waiting in their queue, with the invoice image, the extracted data, the exception and the process history in one screen. From there they can complete their step, refer the invoice to someone else, add a comment or, for AP, change the process type. Filters and personal views make it usable for both a clerk handling a few invoices and an AP team working hundreds a day. On S/4HANA, approvers who are not AP specialists usually work from the Fiori apps instead.

Which transaction for which role

  • AP processor – VIM Workplace for daily work, MIRO/FB60 views for posting checks, FBL1N for vendor items.
  • Approver – VIM Workplace or the Fiori approval app; rarely SAP GUI.
  • AP manager – VIM Analytics for aging, exceptions and productivity.
  • VIM administrator – /OPT/SPRO for configuration, SWI1/SWIA for stuck work items, SM37 for background jobs, WE02 for incoming IDocs.

What to measure: accounts payable KPIs

VIM Analytics gives AP managers and CFOs four key indicators: touchless rate (share of invoices posted with no manual intervention), cycle time from receipt to posting or payment, aging of invoices in queue by exception and owner, and productivity by user, role and company code. The data can also feed SAP Analytics Cloud or other BI tools.

SAP VIM: FAQ

Is SAP VIM an SAP or an OpenText product?

It is developed by OpenText and sold by SAP as an SAP solution extension, so it is licensed and supported through SAP while running inside the SAP system.

Does SAP VIM work with SAP S/4HANA?

Yes. VIM runs on both SAP ECC and SAP S/4HANA, and posts to the same MM-LIV and FI-AP documents. On S/4HANA, approvals and coding are typically done through Fiori apps.

What is the difference between PO and non-PO invoices in VIM?

PO invoices are matched against the purchase order and goods receipt and posted in MM-LIV; non-PO invoices have no order to match, so they are coded and approved through the Chart of Authority and posted in FI-AP.

What is the VIM Workplace transaction code?

/OPT/VIM_WP. It opens the VIM Workplace, the single work queue where every VIM role finds the invoices assigned to it.

How is the approval workflow configured in SAP VIM?

In the VIM IMG (/OPT/SPRO): roles and agent determination decide who receives each step, and the Chart of Authority sets approval limits by company code, amount and expense type. The workflow engine is standard SAP Business Workflow.

Is there a SAP VIM guide in PDF?

Yes. You can download this guide as a PDF for free, and a more detailed 16-page edition in Italian with diagrams and tables.

Do I still need OCR with e-invoicing?

Not for structured e-invoices: the XML already carries the data. OCR remains useful for paper and PDF invoices from suppliers outside the mandate.
